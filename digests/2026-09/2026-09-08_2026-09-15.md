### 1. Informational Help-Seeking on Reddit Did Not Decline After ChatGPT

- [ ] **APPROVE FOR SOCIAL** (pillar: `AI Adoption`)

- **Source:** arXiv: Computers & Society (cs.CY)
- **Link:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12447>
- **Published:** Sep 14, 2026
- **Author(s):** Hazem Ibrahim, Yasir Zaki
- **Keyword score:** 15.48 (AI strong: 14, AI booster: 0, domain: 2)
- **Pillar:** AI Adoption  ·  **Pillar relevance:** 62/100
- **Audience fit:** managers/C-suite: medium · policy makers: medium · general public: high

**Summary (Claude):**

> A study tracking 26 Reddit informational communities against 90 hobby communities finds no meaningful decline in human help-seeking after ChatGPT's launch. The largest defensible upper bound on any decline is 3.4%, far below the 8–25% drops reported in prior work. AI-detector analysis of 274,411 posts and 223,775 comments shows AI-generated content rose only 2–3 percentage points more in informational communities than in hobby ones — not enough to mask a hidden drop in human posts. The authors attribute inflated estimates in earlier studies to a pre-existing divergence between community types that predates ChatGPT. One exception: low-stakes curiosity communities did see an 18% fall in posts, but this matched a trend visible well before ChatGPT existed. Overall, humans continue to ask — and answer — questions for each other online.

**CAMO angle:**

> This study challenges a widely repeated claim: that generative AI has displaced human-to-human information exchange online. From CAMO's perspective on AI adoption, the finding matters because organisational AI rollouts are frequently justified on the premise that AI substitutes for existing human behaviour at scale — a premise this evidence does not support, at least in open peer-advice settings. The methodological lesson is equally relevant: without a concurrent control group, pre-existing trends are routinely misread as AI-driven change, a caution that applies equally to internal adoption metrics inside firms.

**Matched CAMO research:**

- [Deloitte-HKU AI Adoption Index 2026: The Paradox of Promise and Performance](https://camo.hku.hk/ai-adoption-survey/) — Both pieces interrogate the gap between AI's assumed impact and measured behavioural change — the Adoption Index finds shallow organisational transformation despite near-universal deployment, while this study finds no substitution of human help-seeking despite widespread ChatGPT availability.

**Key takeaway:** Despite ChatGPT's availability, humans have not stopped asking other humans for help online — and studies claiming otherwise likely mistook pre-existing trends for an AI effect.

### 2. Voice AI in Firms: A Natural Field Experiment on Automated Job Interviews

**Approve and queue under (tick one)** _(pillar: `AI Adoption`)_:

- **Source:** arXiv: Economics (econ.GN)
- **Link:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28222>
- **Published:** Sep 14, 2026
- **Author(s):** Brian Jabarian, Luca Henkel
- **Keyword score:** 14.51 (AI strong: 8, AI booster: 2, domain: 5)
- **Pillar:** AI Adoption  ·  **Pillar relevance:** 85/100
- **Audience fit:** managers/C-suite: high · policy makers: high · general public: medium

**Summary (Claude):**

> A natural field experiment with 70,000 job applicants tests what happens when AI voice agents replace human recruiters in structured interviews. Applicants randomly assigned to AI interviewers were 12% more likely to receive job offers, and the gains held through job starts and worker retention, with no productivity penalty among those hired. The mechanism appears to be 'adaptive standardization': AI agents conducted more consistent, structured interviews than human recruiters while still responding to individual applicants, producing cleaner, more decision-relevant signals. The authors argue that AI's core advantage in this setting is variance compression — when information collection is distributed across many human workers, individual inconsistency becomes noise, and AI reduces that noise systematically.

**CAMO angle:**

> This study provides unusually clean causal evidence that replacing human interviewers with AI voice agents improves hiring outcomes — not because AI is more empathetic or creative, but because it is more consistent. From CAMO's perspective on AI adoption inside organizations, the implication is that the case for AI automation is strongest precisely where execution is distributed across many human agents whose individual variation degrades collective signal quality. This connects to the broader adoption question of where AI should substitute for human judgment versus augment it: the study suggests that high-volume, structurally repetitive information-collection tasks are prime candidates for substitution, and that organizations underestimate the hidden cost of human variance in these roles.

**Matched CAMO research:**

- [Research: The Hidden Penalty of Using AI at Work](https://camo.hku.hk/2025-02-research-hidden-penalty/) — Both studies examine how AI use in hiring contexts reshapes organizational evaluation and adoption dynamics, though from opposite directions — one showing a competence penalty for workers who use AI, the other showing better outcomes when firms deploy AI as the interviewer.
- [Performance Evaluation and Employee Incentives in the AI Era: Dingdong's Transformation of VOC Assessment](https://camo.hku.hk/nanocase-dingdong/) — Both cases turn on AI's ability to standardize and attribute signals more consistently than distributed human judgment — Dingdong in performance tagging, this study in interview-based information collection.

**Key takeaway:** AI interviewers outperformed human recruiters in a 70,000-applicant experiment by being more consistent, not more capable — a reminder that variance in how humans execute routine tasks is itself an organizational cost.
