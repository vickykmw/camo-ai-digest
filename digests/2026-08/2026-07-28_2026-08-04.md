- **Published:** Aug 03, 2026

- **Pillar:** AI Adoption  ·  **Pillar relevance:** 52/100
- **Audience fit:** managers/C-suite: medium · policy makers: low · general public: medium

**Summary (Claude):**

> How should generative AI platforms price their services without driving users away? This study tests whether ad-supported subscription tiers can help GenAI firms like ChatGPT recover rising operational costs while keeping users engaged. Across four experiments with 1,063 participants, the researchers find that adding a single ad-supported tier nudges free users to upgrade but prompts paying subscribers to downgrade. Offering two ad-supported tiers resolves this tension, retaining paid subscribers while still converting free users. Affective and cognitive evaluations mediate these preferences, and time-intrusive advertising — but not visually intrusive ads — is what most damages user experience. The findings draw on compromise-effect and affective-primacy theories to offer practical pricing-tier design guidance for GenAI platform operators.

**CAMO angle:**

> This study argues that GenAI platform monetization is not simply a pricing problem but a user-psychology problem: the number and sequencing of subscription tiers shapes how users emotionally and cognitively evaluate their options, with real consequences for retention and revenue. From CAMO's perspective on AI adoption, the organisational implication reaches beyond consumer platforms — any firm deploying GenAI tools internally must consider how pricing architecture and access tiers affect whether employees engage seriously with those tools or treat them as disposable. **Key takeaway:** Offering two ad-supported tiers — rather than one — lets GenAI platforms grow paid subscriptions without cannibalising existing ones, because it defuses the compromise effect that otherwise pulls paying users down.
